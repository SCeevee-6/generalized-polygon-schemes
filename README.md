# generalized-polygon-schemes

Association-scheme computations around generalized hexagons and octagons and the Tits group T = 2F4(2)'. Mostly fusions of the point-distance scheme of a generalized polygon, plus orbital algebras and graph searches for T acting on cosets of its subgroups.

Work in progress, not peer reviewed. `fusion/fusion_note.tex` is the draft of the paper.

## Results

- **Fusions.** Take a generalized 2d-gon of order (s,t), d = 3 or 4, with integers s,t >= 2. Its point-distance scheme has a proper fusion iff s = t, and then the fusion is unique: {1,...,d-1} | {d}. Exact proof from the intersection array (Groebner bases over Q, exact real-root isolation, integer scan) in `fusion/certify_fusions.py` and `fusion/certify_note.md`. The hexagon case is known. For octagons I haven't found it in the literature, but I only did keyword searches.
- **Hexagons, s = t = q.** The fusion is strongly regular with parameters ((q^6-1)/(q-1), q(q+1)(q^2+1), q^3+q^2+q-1, (q+1)(q^2+1)), same as the collinearity graph of Q(6,q).
- **Octagons.** s = t is ruled out by Feit-Higman (2st has to be a square), so the Ree-Tits octagon GO(2,4) has no proper fusion.
- **T on 1755 points, (s,t) = (2,4).** Intersection array {10,8,8,8;1,1,1,5}. The eigenvalues of A_1 are 10, 5, 1, -3, -5 with multiplicities 1, 351, 650, 675, 78. The only vanishing non-principal Krein parameter is q^4_44 = 0. Of the 15 fusions of {1,2,3,4} only the two trivial ones close. The scheme is not Q-polynomial in any ordering (De Bruyn-Vanhove 2015) and the graph is not tight (the Jurisic-Koolen-Terwilliger bound reads 0 >= -20, strict; `tits1755/tightness_check.py`). Full write-up in `docs/tits1755_orbital_algebra.md`.
- **Other actions of T.** On cosets of the 8 maximal subgroups the orbital configurations are commutative for degrees 1600 (twice), 1755, 2304 and 2925, and symmetric except for 2304 (L2(25)). They are non-commutative for A6.2^2 (degree 12480, rank 24, twice) and 5^2:4A4 (degree 14976, rank 25). See `tits1755/coherent_configurations_note.md`.
- **Fusions of the commutative actions** (`tits1755/coset_fusions_note.md`). Degree 1600: the one proper fusion is the known SRG(1600,351,94,72) from Crnkovic-Rukavina-Svob (2020). Degree 2304: three fusions (the T.2 orbital scheme of rank 6, its symmetrisation of rank 6, and the symmetrisation of the T.2 scheme of rank 5), none P- or Q-polynomial. Degree 2925: one fusion, the distance scheme of the dual octagon GO(4,2); P- but not Q-polynomial.
- **Non-commutative actions.** The orbital algebras are 11 C + M_2(C) + M_3(C) (degree 12480) and 9 C + 4 M_2(C) (degree 14976). The centre has dimension 13 in both cases, computed exactly from the structure constants and consistent with the character multiplicities.
- **SRG/DRG search for T beyond Crnkovic-Rukavina-Svob** (`search/search_note.md`). Exhaustive search over all unions of orbitals for every T-action of degree 7020 to 17550 (TomLib classes), including the primitive actions on 12480 and 14976 points. No SRG and no DRG of diameter >= 3 on 12480 or 14976 points, so together with CRS 2020 the primitive case is done. On 7020, 8775, 11700 and the 17550-point actions only complete multipartite SRGs show up. No new graph.
- **Brouwer's DRG tables** (`drgfusion/drgfusion_note.md`). Closed fusions of all tabled arrays of diameter <= 7, with the fused SRGs on at most 1300 vertices looked up in Brouwer's SRG table. Nothing new came out: of the six arrays that looked open, two fail the absolute bound, two are deleted in the BCN additions, and two follow from Koval's October 2026 preprint (conditional).
- **Fusions of the non-commutative T schemes** (`search/fusions/fusion_note.md`). The 12480-point configurations have no proper fusion of rank >= 3. Every fusion of rank >= 3 of the 14976-point configuration refines the rank-16 2F4(2) orbital configuration and is non-commutative.
- **Regular near hexagons and octagons** (`fusion/near_polygon_note.md`, `fusion/near_octagon_note.md`). Diameter 3 is BCN 4.2.17. For regular near octagons (s, t2, t3, t) with s >= 2 the integer parameter sets with a proper fusion are: (s, m, s(m+1), (m+1)(s^2+1)-1) with {1,2}|{3,4} (m = s is B_4(s)/C_4(s)), (s,0,0,s), (2, i, 2i, 2i+1) (only the thin octagon (2,1) and H(4,3) are feasible), and H(4,4). This is proved for all thirteen partitions; the three residual surfaces of the first version are closed in `near_octagon_residual.py`. In particular the distance-3 graph is strongly regular only for (2, i, 2i, 2i+1). All examples are known.
- **GO(2,4) triple intersection numbers** (`triple/triple_note.md`). With q^4_44 = 0 the LP ranges only reproduce the octagon geometry for one triple type, so nothing on uniqueness. This zero is the q^2_22 of Bamberg-Lansdown (2023) in their ordering (`krein_convention.py`). T has 415 orbits on ordered triples of points and 2F4(2) has 258 (`triple_orbits.py`); all their triple intersection vectors lie in the LP ranges, ten of the 17 distance types are realised by a single vector, and for six of the other seven the octagon's vectors satisfy linear relations that the LP doesn't imply. The Terwilliger algebra at a point has dimension 174 (exact), against 258 for the centraliser of 2F4(2)_x. The 27-cliques of the distance-<=3 graph are exactly the 2925 line neighbourhoods, which attain the ratio bound; the bound (s+1)(st+1) holds with equality for every order (s,s^2) (`line_cliques.py`, `ratio_bound_family.py`).
- **Maximum non-opposite sets in finite generalized octagons** (`triple/max_nonopposite_proof.md`). In any finite GO of order (s,t), s,t >= 2, a set of pairwise non-opposite points has at most (s+1)(st+1) points (the ratio bound), with equality only for the points at distance at most 1 from a line; dually for lines. Orders with |s-t| = 1 are ruled out by an integrality argument along the way. Hand proof; identities checked symbolically in s and t, and the result confirmed by exhaustive clique enumeration on O(2) and on its dual.
- **Non-opposite sets in no line neighbourhood** (`triple/escape_note.md`). In O(2), every non-opposite set of at least 16 points lies in a line neighbourhood; the 1497600 exceptions of size 15 all consist of a line and 12 points at distance 2 from it, in three T-orbits. In the dual the cutoff is 26 lines, and the 14976 exceptional 25-line families form one orbit with stabiliser 5^2:4A4.
- **The 60 extra triple relations of GO(2,4)** (`triple/triple_relations_note.md`). Exact integer form; 14 of them hold in every GO(2,4) (zeros from a geodesic-tree test, two counts equal to t). For the rest, Ree-Tits triples with the same local configuration have different vectors, so no local argument gives them.
- **Terwilliger algebra at a point** (`triple/terwilliger_note.md`). T(x) is a sum of 15 full matrix algebras over Q (dimension 174); 4 irreducible modules are not thin. Compared block by block with the 258-dimensional centraliser of 2F4(2)_x.
- **The Ree-Tits family GO(q,q^2)** (`family/family_note.md`). The flag scheme is H(D16; q^2, q) of rank 16 for every q, with generic degrees as functions of q (checked against GAP at q = 2); the q = 2 rank-18 splitting comes from the index-2 subgroup 2F4(2)'. For triples, general constraints fix three small types at q = 2 and q = 8 and leave the rest open.
- **T on the 8775 flags** (`flags/flag_note.md`). Rank 18; the 2F4(2)-orbitals are the 16 Weyl-distance relations, with structure constants equal to the Hecke algebra H(D_16; 4, 2). T splits only the two cells where the lines are opposite (stststs and w0). Wedderburn: (m, chi(1)) = (1,1), (1,78), (2,351), (1,624), (1,624'), (2,650), (2,675), (1,2048), (1,2048'), centre of dimension 9 (for 2F4(2): 624 + 624' and 2048 + 2048' merge, centre 7). There are 8 fusions, all of them lifts of the point or line schemes, the 2F4(2) scheme, or trivial ones. No new scheme on 8775 vertices.
- **SRGs on open parameter sets from primitive groups** (`search/primitive/primitive_note.md`). All 2164 primitive groups of rank >= 4 on the 430 open degrees v <= 1300 are settled, and none realises an open parameter set in Brouwer's table (as of 1 Nov 2026). All of it is exact now: the 14 groups that used to rest on floating-point MILPs were redone with integer character sums in CP-SAT (12 affine groups) and a fixed-parameter DFS (the two non-commutative ones).

- **Distance-regular graphs with a primitive group** (`search/drg/drg_note.md`). All 4244 primitive groups (not Sym/Alt) on the 156 degrees below 4096 that carry a primitive BCN array of diameter >= 3. 86 of the 117 arrays that survive the suborbit filter are realised, all by known graphs. For the other 31 no primitive group works: 10 are known not to exist (including {20,16,5;1,1,16}, Makhnev-Golubyatnikov 2022, and {49,40,22;1,5,28}, Gavrilyuk-Vidali, arXiv:2611.02986), one ({44,36,5;1,9,40}) is claimed not to exist by Chen-Makhnev-Klimin 2024 but the last step of their proof uses an identity that fails in known DRGs (see the note), one depends on Koval's preprint, three exist with imprimitive groups (Shi-Krotov-Sole graph, Brouwer graph Br(3), a coset graph on 1024 vertices; the last two built and checked here), and for 16 I found no status. A graph with one of those 16 arrays would have an automorphism group that is not primitive on vertices, so it is either intransitive or imprimitive (a distance-regular graph doesn't have to be vertex-transitive).
- **The 18 open arrays on abelian groups** (`search/cayley/cayley_note.md`; two of the 18 have since turned out to be settled in the literature, see above). None of them is a Cayley graph on an abelian group. 13 fail the Krein test. For the other five, most groups are excluded by Bercov's 1969 theorem on primitive S-rings, plus Shi-Krotov-Sole for the elementary abelian ones. Z25^2 and Z27^2 (and every group with a cyclic factor of order 25, 125 or 27 or more) fall to a one-line atom bound: if p^2 divides the order n of s, the atom of s is invariant under translation by (n/p)s, so phi(n) <= max(a1, c2) (`atom_bound.py`). The remaining groups (Z9^3, Z9^2 x Z3^2, Z10^3) are excluded by the computation (CP-SAT and SCIP). Z9 x Z3^4 also falls under Bercov's theorem, so it was never really open. A run over all 864 nonabelian (group, array) pairs of orders 625, 729, 750 and 1000 (GAP character tables, CP-SAT) shows that none is a normal Cayley graph either (S a union of conjugacy classes). No new graph.

## Reproducing

    pip install -r requirements.txt
    python fusion/verify_fusions.py
    python qpoly/qpoly_check.py
    python tits1755/scheme_from_array.py --atlas
    python code/atlas_orbital_table.py
    python code/bannai_muzychuk_crosscheck.py     # about 7 minutes

The GAP side needs GAP 4.16 with ctbllib and atlasrep (`sh tits1755/gap/run_gap.sh`). `drgfusion/fetch_and_crosscheck.py`, `code/atlas_orbital_table.py`, `triple/go24_triple_lp.py` and the scripts in `flags/` download data, so they need network access (the ATLAS permutations are cached in `flags/atlas_cache/`).

What has been checked on the current commit: `scheme_from_array.py --atlas` exits 0, the 125 p^k_ij from the intersection array equal the counts from the ATLAS permutation action, and the distance in R_1 equals the orbital label at all 1755 points. Both intersection tables in `data/` and `tits1755/out/` agree with it cell by cell. `bannai_muzychuk_crosscheck.py` compiles, but I haven't re-run it since the last change; the CSV is from the previous run.

## Known limitations

- `fusion_note.tex`: the first real compile (pdfTeX, TeX Live 2025 under WSL) stopped on a missing `booktabs.sty`. The preamble now loads booktabs, array, longtable and hyperref only if present. Not recompiled since. Cites and refs resolve and braces balance. The hand proof in Section 3 still uses the sign analysis of eigenvalue differences; the exact proof is cited in Section 7.
- Statements about what is new come from keyword searches (`docs/literature.md`), not a proper review.
- In `coset_schemes.py` the eigenmatrices and Krein parameters are floating point. Fusion tests, intersection numbers and centre dimensions are exact.
- The permutations in `tits1755/cosets/` come from GAP's `FactorCosetAction`, so point numbering depends on GAP and the class labels in `out_cosets/` refer to that numbering.
- Character table checks (GAP 4.16.1, ctbllib 1.3.11, `tits1755/gap/tits_chars.g`): the library table of 2F4(2)' has 22 classes and its degrees square-sum to |T| = 17971200. The permutation character on 1755 points is 1 + 78 + 351 + 650 + 675, multiplicity-free with <pi,pi> = 5, and each eigenspace character computed exactly from Q and the fixed-point counts equals the matching library irreducible. `tits1755/verify_characters.py` repeats this in Python against `gap/tits_chartable.csv`, and a Dixon-Schneider table computed from the generators agrees with the library one (`gap/tits_ctG_output.txt`).
- The two intersection tables in `tits1755/out/` and `data/` have different column names (`p_k_ij` and `p^k_ij`) and the same values, since two scripts write them.

## Opposite components of GO(2,4) and related checks

- **Recognition from one opposite component** (`octagon/`). Rechecked with separate code on both 512-point components of O(2): the bare graph forces the boundary pairing and the whole inward tree, and the reconstructed half contains a full GO(2,1), so De Bruyn's theorem identifies the octagon. The C5-invariant cover count 2,167,056 → 251 → 1 is reproduced by a different method.
- **Component sizes** (`octagon/README.md`). In any GO(2,4) every boundary point lies on exactly |C|/256 lines of each opposite component C, so components have 256, 512, 768 or 1024 points, and the least eigenvalue of every component is at least −13/3.
- **256-point components with 2-group monodromy are impossible** (`octagon/regular256/`), by a spectral window and a tower of double covers. Two implementations reproduce the double and four-sheet record lists (19,132 / 245 / 48 / 27,979; then 16 elementary and 152 cyclic). Independent final lifts give 40 bipartite terminal graphs and exclude the entire cyclic branch even with its spectral filter omitted. Allowing arbitrary intermediate double covers gives 632 four-sheet capacity records, 66 after the spectral test, 106 eight-sheet records, and 64 terminal records, all bipartite.
- **Elementary abelian 512-point components without the five-cycle.** A bare-graph sibling obstruction excludes all 45 saved nonstandard covers (seven isomorphism classes); row 6 is the recognized component. The historical cross-orbit run used an incorrect action on the marked translate. The corrected search (15,086 prefixes, 1.44·10⁹ nodes) leaves 96 covers and the same obstruction excludes all of them, so an elementary abelian regular 512 component is the known one. [Proofs, current status and remaining decompositions](octagon/status.md).
- **Forcing a thin suboctagon from opposite lines.** Six prescribed points of mutual distance three either have no seventh point or extend uniquely to a nine-point grid. One centered transversal then forces a full GO(2,1). All opposite pairs in the Ree–Tits model pass both tests. An integer certificate settles the point-distance marginal test on the initial seed. For a grid with no centered transversal, 26 of 374 direction classes survive exact tests on 81 points. [Proof, checks and remaining existence question](thin/opposite_seed.md).
- **Thin suboctagons** (`thin/overlap_note.md`). One component of the 13-overlap graph has automorphism group exactly T, and four local rules recover the centres and the collinearity of O(2) from the bare graph.
- **Maximum flag families** (`flags/max_family_note.md`). An exact incidence argument identifies the −64 eigenspace for every GO(2,4) as the 2052-dimensional sum pulled back from point and line eigenspaces, so membership in a maximum family has the form a_p + b_L with integer potentials; two occupancy identities follow.
- **Dual packings** (`packing/`). A packing with 133 centres in GO(4,2); upper bound still 223.
- **Smith factor 2** (`smith/`). Point (26, 272) and line (246, 350) benchmarks recomputed through the ker → coker map.
- **Cayley test at order 625** (`search/cayley/trace_note.md`). Second implementation of the trace model; hand proofs for eight of the ten nonabelian groups.
