# The orbital algebra of 2F4(2)' on the 8775 flags of GO(2,4)

## Question

T = 2F4(2)' acts on the 8775 flags (incident point-line pairs) of the Ree-Tits octagon GO(2,4), with a Sylow
2-subgroup (order 2048) as flag stabiliser. This is TomLib class 423, which `search/` already went through for
SRGs and DRGs (rank 18, only complete multipartite graphs). Here the whole configuration is worked out: the
orbitals and their exact intersection numbers, how they sit against the 16 Weyl-distance relations of the
octagon, the Wedderburn decomposition, all fusions, and the maps to the point scheme (1755) and the line
scheme (2925).

## What is known

- For any generalized n-gon the flags with their Weyl distance (2n relations) form a coherent configuration
  (Higman, Invariant relations, coherent configurations and generalized polygons, in Combinatorics, 1975,
  pp. 347-363, doi 10.1007/978-94-010-1826-5_18). For a group with a split BN-pair the orbitals on chambers are
  the Bruhat cells and the adjacency algebra is the Iwahori-Hecke algebra. So the 2F4(2) part below is
  standard; the computation only checks it.
- Leonard, Using Groebner bases to investigate flag algebras and association scheme fusion, in Algorithmic
  Algebraic Combinatorics and Groebner Bases, Springer 2009, pp. 113-135 (doi 10.1007/978-3-642-01960-9_3),
  sets up the flag algebra of a generalized polygon from two generators and looks for fusions with Groebner
  bases. Colangelo, Monzillo and Siciliano, Discrete Math. 347 (2024) 114054 (doi 10.1016/j.disc.2024.114054),
  work out the flag scheme of a generalized quadrangle and all its fusions. I have not read Leonard's chapter,
  only the description of it in Colangelo-Monzillo-Siciliano, so I can't say whether his fusion list covers
  octagons of order (2,4).
- I did not find the T-orbital configuration on flags written down anywhere (keyword searches only).

## Method

`flag_scheme.py`, from the ATLAS permutations of 2F4(2) on 1755 points (`go24_common.py`; T is generated by
c and (cdcdcdcd)^(dcddcdcddd)).

1. Lines are the triangles of the collinearity graph (2925), flags the 8775 incident pairs; T and 2F4(2) act
   on flags through their action on points and lines.
2. Stabiliser of a flag in T from Schreier generators, with order 2048 checked by sympy. Orbitals and the
   p^k_ij by `tits1755/coset_schemes.py` (exact integers).
3. Weyl distance: with sigma = 'same point, other line' and tau = 'same line, other point', the relation of
   w in D_16 is the support of A_{s1}...A_{sk} along a reduced word; each support is checked to be 0/1 (unique
   reduced galleries) and the 16 supports partition the flags.
4. 2F4(2)-orbitals as unions of T-orbitals.
5. The 16 Weyl-distance relations against the Iwahori-Hecke algebra of D_16 with q_sigma = t = 4,
   q_tau = s = 2 (T_w T_r = T_wr if l(wr) > l(w), else q_r T_wr + (q_r - 1) T_w), all 16^3 structure constants.
6. Centre (exact nullspace), central idempotents from a generic central element (exact, sympy, with the
   quadratic irrationalities), m_i = sqrt of the component dimension, and chi_i(1) = n e_0 / m_i from the trace
   of the idempotent on C^8775.
7. Fusions: the coarsest fusion in which a given union of classes is a union of classes (algebraic WL on the
   structure constants, transposes included) for all 2^16 unions up to complement, then closed under meets.
   Every fusion F is the meet of the closures of its own classes, so this gives all of them. Each one found is
   checked directly against the fusion condition.

Runtime about 7 minutes (the fusion enumeration is most of it). Output: `out/flag_scheme_output.txt`,
`out/flag_scheme.json`, `out/flag_intersection_numbers.csv`.

## Result

T on flags has rank 18 with suborbit lengths 1, 2, 4, 8, 8, 16, 32, 64, 64, 128, 256, 512, 512, 1024, 1024,
1024, 2048, 2048. 12 classes are symmetric and the algebra is not commutative.

- 2F4(2) on flags has rank 16, and its orbitals are exactly the 16 Weyl-distance relations. Their structure
  constants agree with the Hecke algebra H(D_16; 4, 2) in all 4096 places.
- T splits exactly two Weyl cells, each into two halves: stststs (length 7, 2048 = 1024 + 1024) and w0
  (length 8, 4096 = 2048 + 2048). In both cells the two lines are opposite (distance 4 in the dual octagon).
  This matches the line action: T has rank 6 on the 2925 lines and 2F4(2) rank 5, the opposite lines being
  the orbit that splits (`tits1755/coset_fusions_note.md`).
- Wedderburn decomposition, as (m_i, chi_i(1)) with m_i the size of the matrix block and chi_i the
  corresponding irreducible constituent of the permutation character:
  - 2F4(2): (1,1), (1,78), (2,351), (2,650), (2,675), (1,1248), (1,4096); centre of dimension 7. This is the
    shape of the Hecke algebra of D_16 (4 linear and 3 two-dimensional representations); 4096 is the Steinberg
    character.
  - T: (1,1), (1,78), (2,351), (1,624), (1,624), (2,650), (2,675), (1,2048), (1,2048); centre of dimension 9.
    So 1248 restricts to T as 624 + 624' and 4096 as 2048 + 2048', and the other constituents stay
    irreducible. All nine degrees are T character degrees (the list is in the README, from the GAP library
    table), and sum m_i chi_i(1) = 8775.
- Fusions of the T configuration (8 in total):
  - rank 18 and rank 16 (the 2F4(2) scheme);
  - rank 7, valencies 1, 2, 36, 288, 2304, 3072, 3072: 'same flag', 'same line', and then the line distance in the
    dual octagon, with the opposite lines split as in the rank-6 T-action on lines. This is the only fusion
    that is not a fusion of the 2F4(2) scheme;
  - rank 6, valencies 1, 2, 36, 288, 2304, 6144: the same with the T.2 line scheme;
  - rank 6, valencies 1, 4, 50, 400, 3200, 5120: 'same point', then the point distance (point scheme of the
    octagon);
  - rank 3: the two complete multipartite schemes (blocks = the 5 flags on a point, or the 3 flags on a line);
  - rank 2.
- Fusions of the 2F4(2) scheme (6): itself, the two rank-6 lifts of the point and line schemes, the two complete
  multipartite rank-3 schemes, and rank 2.
- Projection of each T-orbital to (point distance, line distance) is in `out/flag_scheme_output.txt`. The pair
  separates all Weyl distances except st/ts, stst/tsts and ststst/tststs, which are mutually transposed. In the
  rank-7 fusion each class joins one half of stststs with one half of w0.

## What this says

On the flags, T sees nothing that is not already in the Hecke algebra of 2F4(2) plus the rank-6 T-action on
lines. Every fusion is one of the obvious ones: the 2F4(2) scheme, a lift of the point scheme or of a line
scheme, or a complete multipartite scheme. In particular there is no fusion that mixes point and line
information in a new way, so the flag configuration gives no new strongly regular graph or association scheme
on 8775 vertices (consistent with the SRG/DRG search in `search/`). The extra structure T has on flags is just
the splitting of the opposite-line cells, which comes from 2048 + 2048' and 624 + 624'.

Known: the 2F4(2) part (Higman; Bruhat decomposition and Hecke algebra). The T-orbital configuration with its
Wedderburn decomposition and fusion list: I found it nowhere, but it follows quickly from known facts, so it is
a table, not a theorem.

## What rests on what

- ATLAS permutations of 2F4(2) on 1755 points, and the word for T inside 2F4(2) from the ATLAS page.
- sympy for the order of the flag stabiliser (2048) and for the exact Wedderburn computation.
- The fusion enumeration is exact; it relies on the closure argument above (every fusion is the meet of the
  closures of its classes).
